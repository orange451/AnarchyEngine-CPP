# Close Roblox's simulation gap, keep its simplicity

Roblox's ParticleEmitter has kept getting better at how particles look (procedural shapes in 2021, flipbooks in 2022, Squash), but its simulation has hardly changed. For about a decade creators have asked for the same things: **particle collisions, 3D/mesh particles, sub-emitters and events, more blend modes and real lighting, turbulence and richer motion, and an end to silent quality throttling**. Every other engine surveyed has already solved these. The best model for AnarchyEngine is not Niagara's graph or VFX Graph's compute pipeline. It is the fixed-function core those engines converge on: Unity Shuriken's module stack, Epic's own 17-module "Lightweight" Niagara emitter, Godot's flat ParticleProcessMaterial, and Defold's curve-keyed emitter. Almost everything in that core is cheap per-particle arithmetic on the CPU. The items that cost real money are world collision, trails (they need a second geometry path), per-particle lights and high-octave noise. The modern look comes mostly from the renderer, not the simulation: premultiplied blending, HDR emission into the existing bloom, soft particles from the deferred depth buffer, and flipbook frame blending each add a large visual gain for a few shader instructions on OpenGL 3.3. The shortlist at the end proposes a must-have tier that answers Roblox's top complaints without per-particle scripting or a node graph.

## Roblox shipped looks for ten years and left simulation alone

Today's ParticleEmitter is a billboard sprite system. It has per-lifetime Color, Size, Transparency and Squash curves, four procedural shapes (Box, Sphere, Cylinder, Disc), flipbooks, Acceleration, exponential Drag, wind, velocity inheritance, a 0-to-1 LightEmission slider between alpha and additive, and `Emit(n)` bursts. It has **no events, no collisions, no mesh particles and no sub-emitters** ([ParticleEmitter reference](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ParticleEmitter)). The hard caps are tight. Lifetime is clamped to **20 s**, a single emitter makes at most **400 particles/s (100/s on mobile)**, and flipbooks top out at **30 fps** on square power-of-two textures no larger than 1024x1024 ([Particle Emitters guide](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters); [Flipbook release](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particles’-flipbook-release/2029388)). Only the four curves and the force terms change over a particle's life. Speed, Rotation and RotSpeed are rolled once at spawn, so there is no velocity-over-lifetime, orbital, radial, noise or attractor control. Changing Rate, Speed or Lifetime does not affect particles already alive ([ParticleEmitter.md](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ParticleEmitter.md)). Some of the semantics also trip creators up. LightInfluence defaults to 1 when the emitter is made in Studio but 0 when made with `Instance.new()`. TimeScale can only slow particles down, never speed them up. Sphere and Cylinder shapes misbehave under an Attachment parent, because shape size comes from the parent part's bounding box ([ParticleEmitter.md](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ParticleEmitter.md); [Shapes announcement](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/new-procedural-particle-emitter-shapes/1517642)).

Ranked by how often they recur and how much engagement they get, the complaints follow a clear order. **Collisions come first.** The most-liked reply on Roblox's 2021 shapes announcement (40 likes) asked for them, and the request returns in 2021 and 2025 overhaul threads. Several community modules exist only to add them ([Shapes announcement](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/new-procedural-particle-emitter-shapes/1517642); [VFX system update features](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/vfx-system-update-features/1293316); [Particle System Revamp/Overhaul](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particle-system-revampoverhaul/4005723); [Collideable Particles Module](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/collideable-particles-module/2279402)). **Mesh/3D particles** and particles that do not face the camera come second. Creators currently fake them by creating, tweening and destroying instances, or with plugins such as Sine VFX ([t/4005723](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particle-system-revampoverhaul/4005723); [Sine VFX](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/plugin-sine-vfx-a-vfx-plugin-for-roblox-studio/4803771)). Next come **sub-emitters and triggers** for rain splashes, fireworks and explosions, then **multiply/subtractive blending, shadow casting and light-emitting particles**, then **turbulence, speed-driven size and color, rate over distance and native bursts** ([t/1293316](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/vfx-system-update-features/1293316)). Performance complaints form their own category. One 2025 thread, with disputed numbers, saw a close-up aura emitter take a game from 240 to 60 fps ([Improve the performance of ParticleEmitters](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/improve-the-performance-of-particleemitters/3547121)). Users also report that the engine quietly lowers the Rate of enabled emitters at low graphics settings with no override, so artists call `Emit()` in a loop because bursts are not throttled ([How could I bypass this?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-could-i-bypass-this/1994624)). The sorting gripe is narrower: background Beams draw over particles depending on view angle, which staff called by design but put on the backlog ([Background Beams overriding ParticleEmitters](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/background-beams-overriding-particleemitters/3664511)).

Each workaround points at a missing feature. Script bursts stand in for burst scheduling, stacked rate-1 emitters for multi-stage effects, tweened MeshParts for mesh particles, rings of Attachments for arbitrary emission shapes, and script-moved Attachments for particle lights ([t/1293316](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/vfx-system-update-features/1293316); [t/3985524](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particleemitter-from-certain-part-of-part/3985524)). Two of Roblox's numbers are unverified and should not be treated as targets: the commonly quoted 20-keypoint sequence limit and a "16,000 desktop / 3,600 mobile" global budget. No source could be found for either.

## Every mature engine converges on a fixed-function core

The engines split into two families. One is the "fat emitter": a single component whose properties are constant, random or keyed over life (Roblox, Defold, Effekseer, Unity Shuriken). The other is a modular stack or graph (Source 2's emitters/initializers/operators/renderers, s&box, Niagara, PopcornFX, Bevy Hanabi's expression modules) ([Defold manual](https://defold.com/manuals/particlefx/); [VDC: HL:Alyx Particles](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Half-Life:_Alyx_Workshop_Tools/Particles); [bevy_hanabi README](https://github.com/djeedai/bevy_hanabi)). The telling fact is that the graph-based engines have themselves moved back toward fixed function. **Epic's UE 5.4+ Lightweight emitters are a fixed set of about 17 modules**: acceleration, add velocity, curl noise, drag, gravity, shape location, rotate around point, size and color scaling including by speed, sprite rotation rate and Sub UV animation. Users "cannot create custom modules, scratchpads, or use dynamic inputs", and in return there are no scripts or per-particle data in memory and lower game-thread and render-thread cost ([Epic: Lightweight Emitters Overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/niagara-lightweight-emitters-overview?lang=en-US)). Godot made the same move from the other direction. Its ParticleProcessMaterial is one flat property sheet covering spawn and update, which works as a ready-made template for a property panel ([ParticleProcessMaterial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_particleprocessmaterial.html)).

| System | Model | Sim | What AnarchyEngine should take from it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unity Shuriken | ~23 optional modules on a Main module | CPU, native | MinMaxCurve value model; bursts with probability; orbital/radial velocity; sub-emitters on birth/death/collision; ring-buffer mode; procedural culling |
| Unity VFX Graph | Node graph, compute | GPU only, no GLES | Point-cache spawning, GPU events as an idea, six-way smoke lighting |
| Unreal Niagara | Programmable stack; Lightweight fixed subset | CPU or GPU | Lightweight module list; Effect Type scalability; sprite cutout; ribbon model (RibbonID + link order) |
| Godot 4 | Flat ParticleProcessMaterial | GPU (CPU path frozen) | Flat property sheet; `amount_ratio`; `fixed_fps` + `interpolate` + `fract_delta`; hide-on-contact collision |
| Defold | Fat emitter with curves | CPU | Curves over emitter life *and* particle life; Radial/Vortex modifiers; Alpha/Add/Multiply/Screen |
| Source 2 / s&box | Function stack | CPU | Shows the operator-list shape for later; trace collision flagged as expensive |
| Effekseer | Standalone node editor | Many runtimes | Designer-owned authoring with real-time preview; added turbulence and expressions in 1.5 |
| Bevy Hanabi | Expressions on compute | GPU | Plane/box/sphere colliders, kill boxes, screen-space size, velocity stretch |

Unity's most transferable idea is not any one module. It is **MinMaxCurve**: every scalar is Constant, Curve, Random Between Two Constants, or Random Between Two Curves. One widget therefore covers fixed values, change over time and per-particle variety ([ParticleSystemCurveMode](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/ParticleSystemCurveMode.html)). Unity's own fast path assumes short curves (no more than 8 segments for procedural mode), which supports baking each curve into a small lookup table ([Unity blog: Culling tips](https://unity.com/blog/engine-platform/particlesystem-performance-culling-tips)). AnarchyEngine already uses NumberRange and NumberSequence-style properties, so adding "random between two sequences" is an incremental change, not a new concept. Defold adds the second axis that Roblox lacks entirely: curves keyed over the emitter's duration (spawn rate, initial speed, initial size) as well as over the particle's life ([Defold manual](https://defold.com/manuals/particlefx/)). That is how a one-shot explosion ramps its spawn rate without scripting.

Shuriken's module inventory also maps out the motion vocabulary Roblox lacks. Velocity over Lifetime adds linear, **orbital** and **radial** components around a chosen center, plus a speed modifier. Limit Velocity adds drag scaled by size or speed. Noise has strength, frequency, scroll and octaves. Emission has **Rate over Distance** and bursts with Count, Cycles, Interval and Probability ([VelocityOverLifetimeModule](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/ParticleSystem.VelocityOverLifetimeModule.html); [Emission module](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/PartSysEmissionModule.html); [Noise module](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/PartSysNoiseModule.html)). Godot's equivalents include tangential acceleration, damping-as-friction and a velocity limit that exists because animated velocities otherwise ignore damping ([ParticleProcessMaterial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_particleprocessmaterial.html)). These are the most frequently requested "advanced motion" features on the Roblox forums. On a structure-of-arrays CPU loop each costs a few vector operations per particle.

The counter-evidence matters too. Godot froze its CPU particle path in 4.2 because of "high maintenance cost and bug surface", and the frozen path lacks turbulence, sub-emitters, trails, attractors and collision ([Godot blog, Oct 2023](https://godotengine.org/article/progress-report-state-of-particles/)). That is a warning about maintaining two simulation backends side by side. It is not evidence that CPU simulation can't do these features: Shuriken does all of them on the CPU and remains Unity's recommendation below a few thousand particles. One Unity developer found Shuriken "can definitely outperform" VFX Graph on weak GPUs ([Choosing your particle system](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/ChoosingYourParticleSystem.html); [Unity forum](https://forum.unity.com/threads/should-i-allow-fallback-to-shuriken-particles-for-lower-end-gpu-bound-systems.892072/)). Epic's own guidance agrees that GPU-limited platforms may favor CPU simulation, and that a one-particle GPU sim can cost as much as a 64-particle one ([Epic best practices](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/scalability-and-best-practices-for-niagara)).

## Collision, sub-emitters and noise fit a CPU budget if tiered

Collision is the top Roblox request, and the other engines show that users accept approximate collision. Unity offers cheap **Planes** collision (a dot product per plane) and **World** collision with three quality tiers. The Medium and Low tiers cache static geometry in a voxel grid reused across frames, at the price of occasional pass-through ([Collision module](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/PartSysCollisionModule.html)). Godot's GPU particles never touch the physics world. They collide only with box, sphere, heightfield or baked-SDF collider nodes, and offer a **"hide on contact"** mode beside rigid bounce ([Godot collision tutorial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/particles/collision.html)). s&box flags trace-based collision as expensive and to be used sparingly ([s&box wiki](https://wiki.facepunch.com/sbox/Understanding_Emmiters_Initializers_&_Operators)). For AnarchyEngine the sensible ladder is: a ground plane or analytic planes, boxes and spheres (nearly free); then a terrain heightfield lookup (one bilinear sample per particle); then capped raycasts against Box3D with cached hit planes reused between casts, budgeted per frame. The ladder is engineering judgement built from these precedents, not a measured result. Combined with kill-on-contact and a sub-emitter fired on collision, it covers sparks, rain splashes and debris, which is most of what Roblox creators build collision modules for.

Sub-emitters are cheap on a CPU. A parent's death or collision appends a spawn request to a queue that the child drains on the next tick. Unity supports Birth, Collision, Death, Trigger and Manual conditions, each with an emit probability and inherit flags ([Sub Emitters module](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/PartSysSubEmitModule.html)). Godot's rule is worth copying: a child's live count is capped by its own amount, so chains can go to any depth without blowing the budget ([Godot sub-emitters](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/particles/subemitters.html)). A Manual trigger lets scripts fire stages without per-particle access, so it also replaces most of Roblox's script-burst workarounds.

Turbulence is the one motion feature with a real CPU price. Unity warns that extra octaves "significantly add" cost ([Noise module](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/PartSysNoiseModule.html)). Godot warns that its 3D noise has "a high performance cost" and suggests a vector-field attractor sampled from a 3D noise texture as the cheaper route ([Godot turbulence tutorial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/particles/turbulence.html)). Curl noise gives divergence-free, fluid-looking swirls without a solver ([Curl-noise for procedural fluid flow](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=115355)). No source benchmarks CPU curl-noise cost. The safe design is to bake a small tiling curl-noise velocity grid (for example 16³ or 32³) once, sample it trilinearly per particle, and expose Strength (as a curve), Frequency and Scroll. That makes cost independent of octave count.

Two timing details carry over to a 240 Hz physics engine with variable-rate rendering. Godot simulates particles on their own clock (`fixed_fps`, default 30). It interpolates between the last two states at render time, and uses `fract_delta` to give particles emitted mid-step sub-step start times so fast emitters don't clump into bands ([GPUParticles3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gpuparticles3d.html)). Godot's issue #50824 shows the pitfall: interpolation that ignores time scale produces visible jitter, especially with orbital motion ([Godot issue #50824](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/50824)). Particles are visual-only, so they should not run at 240 Hz. They should run at render rate, or at a lower fixed rate with interpolation, and always spread spawn times and positions along the emitter's path within a step.

## The modern look is a renderer problem GL 3.3 already solves

On a fill-rate-bound low-end GPU, overdraw is the real limit, not particle count. PowerVR names stacked alpha-blended quads as a leading cause of overdraw even with few, large particles ([Imagination: Optimal Particle Rendering](https://docs.imgtec.com/performance-guides/graphics-recommendations/html/topics/optimal-particle-rendering-on-powervr.html)). Roblox's own aura-emitter complaint shows the same thing. Humus found that **trimming quads to each texture's alpha bounds alone saved over 30%** of fill, with little gain beyond about 8 vertices ([Humus](https://www.humus.name/index.php?ID=266)). Niagara exposes the same idea as a sprite cutout texture with a bounding mode ([NiagaraSpriteRendererProperties](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/NiagaraSpriteRendererProperties)). With instancing, the trimmed polygon is one shared 4-to-8-vertex buffer per texture, so the saving costs nothing per instance. Off-screen low-resolution particle buffers go further for very heavy scenes ([GPU Gems 3 ch.23](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iv-image-effects/chapter-23-high-speed-screen-particles)).

Four shader-level features account for most of the visual gain. **Premultiplied alpha** (blend ONE, ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA) lets additive fire and occluding smoke share one batch with no state change ([Wicked Engine](https://wickedengine.net/2017/10/which-blend-state-for-me); [Anisoptera Games](https://www.anisopteragames.com/hdr-effects/)). Roblox's LightEmission slider maps directly onto it, provided "additiveness" is a separate scalar from opacity, because otherwise lowering alpha makes a particle more additive instead of more transparent ([polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/125927/shader)). Multiply and screen, which Defold ships and Roblox creators request, need their own blend state and so their own batch ([Defold manual](https://defold.com/manuals/particlefx/)). **HDR emission** above 1.0 written into the transparency target feeds the existing bloom, provided transparency is composited before the bright-pass ([Anisoptera Games](https://www.anisopteragames.com/hdr-effects/)). **Soft particles** fade sprites where they meet geometry instead of showing a hard intersection line ([NVIDIA Soft Particles](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/whitepapers/2007/SDK10/SoftParticles_hi.pdf)). The deferred renderer already has the depth texture, so the cost is one fetch per fragment. **Flipbook frame blending** is a one-line lerp, and Roblox already has it. Motion-vector blending can make a 16-frame flipbook look as smooth as a 36-frame one, but only with authored flow maps ([realtimevfx](https://realtimevfx.com/t/motion-vectors-for-animated-textures-how-do-you-generate-them/575?page=2)).

Lighting has a clear ladder. The base is Roblox's LightInfluence blend between unlit and lit, evaluated once per particle from ambient and sun, with a sun shadow-map sample in the vertex shader for receiving shadows. **Six-way lightmaps** pre-bake smoke lit from six directions into two RGBA textures and blend them by scene lights at runtime. Unity positions them for background smoke, steam and explosions ([Unity six-way lighting](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.visualeffectgraph@17.0/manual/six-way-lighting.html)). That costs two fetches, but needs authored assets. Particle point lights are possible but must be hard-capped: Unity's Lights module has a Maximum Lights field because too many lights make the game "run very slowly" ([Lights module](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/PartSysLightsModule.html)).

Render modes and sorting complete the renderer. Every surveyed engine offers billboard, velocity-stretched, axis-locked and world/local-oriented quads, all computable in the instanced vertex shader from position, velocity and size ([Renderer module](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/PartSysRendererModule.html); [bevy_hanabi](https://github.com/djeedai/bevy_hanabi)). Instanced mesh particles answer Roblox's second-biggest request and fit GL 3.3 instancing directly. Sorting stays per emitter everywhere: Niagara leaves it off by default, and `sort_order_hint` orders whole emitters ([Epic best practices](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/scalability-and-best-practices-for-niagara)). Spawn order in a ring buffer gives age sorting for free and stable indices for motion vectors, which is the only draw order Godot allows with TAA ([GPUParticles3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gpuparticles3d.html)). Trails are the expensive outlier. Godot's skinned trails are missing from its own GL Compatibility renderer ([Godot trails](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/particles/trails.html)). The GL 3.3 path is a CPU-built camera-facing strip from a point history in a dynamic VBO. Niagara's RibbonID plus link-order model lets one data structure serve both "ribbon through the emitter's particles" and "trail behind each particle" ([UNiagaraRibbonRendererProperties](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.26/API/Plugins/Niagara/UNiagaraRibbonRendererProperties/index.html)).

## Budgets should be visible and global, not silent and per-emitter

Roblox's throttling frustrates creators because it is invisible and can't be overridden, and its per-emitter Rate cap pushes them toward unthrottled `Emit()` loops. Under the "it just works" principle, AnarchyEngine should drop arbitrary caps like the 400/s rate and the 20 s lifetime, and move protection to a global level the user can see. Niagara's Effect Types show the full version: per-quality distance culling, instance caps kept by significance, global budget scaling and visibility culling ([NiagaraSystemScalabilitySettings](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/NiagaraSystemScalabilitySettings)). Godot's `amount_ratio` shows the minimal version: a runtime emission multiplier that leaves live particles alone and does not restart the system ([GPUParticles3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gpuparticles3d.html)). A per-emitter MaxParticles, a global rate multiplier driven by quality and total live count, distance culling, and frustum culling against computed bounds give the same protection without hidden behavior. Script `Emit()` should go through the same budget, so it stops being a loophole.

Unity's procedural mode is the cheapest scalability win for a CPU sim. If a particle moves only under constant acceleration and life curves, its state at any time is a closed-form function of age. Off-screen emitters can then skip simulation entirely and catch up instantly ([Unity blog: Culling tips](https://unity.com/blog/engine-platform/particlesystem-performance-culling-tips)). Niagara's stateless emitters follow the same reasoning ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/niagara-lightweight-emitters-overview?lang=en-US)). The same closed form makes prewarm free instead of the hitch Epic and Godot both warn about ([Epic best practices](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/scalability-and-best-practices-for-niagara); [GPUParticles3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gpuparticles3d.html)). Drag, noise, collision, world space and sub-emitters drop the emitter to stepped integration. Unity's inspector lists exactly which features broke the fast path, and AnarchyEngine's property panel could do the same. Seeding each emitter's RNG deterministically (Unity's Random Seed) makes effects repeatable and lets networked clients reproduce bursts by replicating only seed and start time ([Main module](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/PartSysMainModule.html)).

## Prioritized feature shortlist

Cost tags: **CPU** is the per-particle or per-emitter simulation cost. **GL** is the GL 3.3 render cost or render-path complexity. Each is rated Low, Medium or High. Ratings are engineering judgement grounded in the sources above, not benchmarks.

| Sub-emitters on birth/death/collision/manual, child capped by its own max | #3 Roblox request; fireworks, splashes | Low | Low (more draws) |

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the problem. Building a better emitter than Roblox's is not about matching Niagara or VFX Graph. Epic and Godot both drifted back to fixed-function property sheets because programmable systems cost memory, iteration time and maintenance. The real gap is that Roblox stopped at appearance. Nearly everything its creators rebuild in Luau (collisions, sub-emitters, orbital and noise motion, bursts, mesh particles) is cheap CPU arithmetic, while nearly everything that makes particles look current (premultiplied HDR, soft edges, blended flipbooks, trimmed quads) is a handful of GL 3.3 shader instructions on infrastructure AnarchyEngine already has. Only a few features carry real cost: world raycast collision, trails, lights, shadows and turbulence. Each has a known cheap approximation, so none of them needs to block the must-have tier.

Two design choices matter more than any single feature. The first is to make the closed-form, stateless path the default and treat stateful features as opt-ins the panel names explicitly; that gives free prewarm, free off-screen culling and network-reproducible effects. The second is to replace Roblox's hidden per-emitter caps and quality throttling with one visible global budget that also governs scripted bursts. Together they let AnarchyEngine drop the limits Roblox creators resent while still protecting low-end hardware. What remains unknown is the actual CPU cost of turbulence and collision at AnarchyEngine's particle counts. No source benchmarks them, so they should be profiled on a Release build before their tier placement is finalized.
